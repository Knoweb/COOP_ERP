# m5inventory — M5 Inventory, Costing, Repack & Loss

The living guide of the module (AGENTS.md): where stock is and what it cost, by location, batch and condition. Once code exists, this file and the tests supersede 25A for day-to-day work; every deviation from the guide is listed at the end with its reason. Read `hello/README.md` first: its six rules apply here unchanged.

Built so far, for the demo (Phase 1: the Federation selling to a distributor): M5-01 (schema), M5-02 (the ledger), M5-04 (the queries, and M5's answer to M2's lot questions), M5-03 (the GRN and delivery consumers), M5-10 (opening balances) and, for phase 3 (the shop), M5-09 (transfers). The demo-minimal scope and what was deferred are in `docs/PROGRESS.md` and under "Deferred after the demo" in `docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md`.

## The one rule

**`LedgerService.post` is the only place stock changes** (25A "Read this first"). Receipts, sales, counts, write-offs, repacks, transfers and opening balances are callers: each assembles `Movement`s, cites its document and calls `StockLedger.post` inside its own command. Nothing else writes `stock_lot.qty_on_hand`, `stock_movement` or `entity_sku_cost`; the database grants make most of that impossible anyway (below).

## The ledger (25A section 6.1)

`post(PostMovements(documentId, occurredAt, occurredLocal, movements), scope)`:

1. **Guards**: an OWN scope with an entity (`m5.scope.own_required`); a document (`m5.ledger.document_required`); at least one movement (`m5.ledger.movements_required`); each movement complete (`m5.ledger.movement_incomplete`), its quantity non-zero with at most three decimals (`m5.ledger.qty_invalid`), its sign the one its type allows (`m5.ledger.sign_invalid`: RECEIPT, OPENING_BALANCE, REPACK_PRODUCE, TRANSFER_IN and SALE_REVERSAL add; COUNT_ADJUST either way; the rest take away), its cost given for a type that carries one (`m5.ledger.cost_required`, at most four decimals `m5.ledger.cost_invalid`); each batch known to M2 (`m5.batch.not_found`); each location one of the scope entity's that the scope reads through M1 (`m5.location.not_in_scope`: a shop session posts at its own shop only). **A negative lot is not refused**: an offline oversell takes the lot below zero and the record shows what the till believed (doc 25 section 3.2).
2. **Locks, in one order**, so two postings cannot deadlock: every lot the posting touches, created if missing, in the order of its identity (location, batch, condition); then the movement numbers, a block per location in location order; then the entity's cost rows in SKU order.
3. **Movements, in the order given**: each gets its number (dense per location and source; the source is `central` or the device id), its cost (`costFor`: the caller's for an intake, a transfer in or a sale reversal, and for an out movement the caller gives a cost; the entity average otherwise), moves its lot, and moves the entity average.
4. **Audit** `STOCK_POSTED` for the document; `STOCK_LOT_NEGATIVE` (REVIEW) when a lot goes below zero, `STOCK_LOT_NEGATIVE_CLEARED` when it comes back, each judged by the lot's sign when locked against its sign at the end of the posting: a lot negative before and after keeps its `negative_since` and records nothing (wave 2, M5-07). **Events** `stock.moved.v1` per movement, `lot.negative.v1` per lot that went below zero.

A lot created by a movement that carries no cost of its own (a sale against a lot the location never had, doc 25 flow 6.2) starts at the entity average.

### The weighted average (`CostService`; doc 25 section 3.3)

Per (entity, SKU), since wave 2 (CR-25A-1 item 5; `docs/progress/deviations/2026-10-06-wave2-stock-movements.md` (5)): a movement that carries its own cost (RECEIPT, OPENING_BALANCE, REPACK_PRODUCE, TRANSFER_IN, SALE_REVERSAL; `MovementType.carriesItsOwnCost`) re-averages, `(qty × avg + qtyIn × costIn) / (qty + qtyIn)`, cost scale 4, half up. A TRANSFER_IN comes back at the cost its TRANSFER_OUT left with (`transfer_line.unit_cost`), so the pair conserves value exactly and a GRN that lands while stock is in transit is averaged against the right base (M5-06: 100 at 10 all in transit, 50 at 20 received, then the transfer in: 13.3333, where the old rule kept 20). A SALE_REVERSAL comes back at the original SALE's `unit_cost_at_movement` (D10; declared, posted by no one until the till's voids and refunds). An out movement the caller gives a cost leaves at that cost, `(qty × avg − qtyOut × costOut) / (qty − qtyOut)` while something remains, else the last average, never below zero: the repack reversal takes the packs out at `repack.output_unit_cost` (M5-17), so the output item's average returns to what it was before the repack. Every other movement changes the quantity and keeps the average (the last known one at zero). **Rounding:** a repack's output cost is the consumed value over the packs made, rounded to four decimals; the remainder (at most Rs 0.00005 a pack, Rs 0.05 per 1,000 packs) is not carried anywhere, as every average-cost movement rounds the same way (accepted in wave 2, M5-17). When the entity holds nothing or owes stock (after an oversell), an intake's cost becomes the average: there is nothing to average with, and averaging with a negative quantity gives a meaningless or negative figure (25A: "avg never negative").

### What the database refuses the application

`stock_movement`: SELECT and INSERT only. `stock_lot`: SELECT, INSERT and UPDATE of `qty_on_hand`, `last_movement_seq`, `negative_since`, `negative_acknowledged_at` only, so a lot's identity and acquisition cost never change. `entity_sku_cost`, `movement_sequence`: UPDATE of their counters only. Nothing is ever deleted (`InventorySchemaIntegrationTest`). Row-level security follows the template on every table (`RlsMatrixIntegrationTest` covers them by their owner column): a shop session reads and writes its own shop's rows; `entity_sku_cost` has no location, since a shop's sale moves the entity's average.

## The reads (M5-04; 25A sections 5 and 7)

`query.InventoryQueries` (`internal/availability/InventoryQueriesImpl`), read-only, in the caller's scope under row-level security:

| Query | Answer |
|---|---|
| `balances(location, sku?, includeZero)` | The lots of a location; GOOD lots with stock first, in FEFO order: `fefoRank` 1 is the lot to sell or pick first, ranked by expiry (none last), then by when it was received, then its id; DAMAGED, empty and negative lots have no rank. `GET /v1/inventory/locations/{id}/balances` (`inv.stock.view`): the unit cost only for an owner's user, never for a till or a read-only class. |
| `availability(locations, skus)` | Per requested pair: the GOOD lots with stock, never a negative lot, less the undispatched reservations of issued delivery notes (with M5-03); zero where there is nothing. `GET /v1/inventory/availability` (`inv.stock.view`). |
| `pickBatches`, `inStockBatches` | The GOOD lots with stock in FEFO order, at one location or several (M4's delivery note, M3's authoring checks). |
| `skusWithLots`, `entityAverageCost`, `lotsConsumed(grn)` | M2's assortment; the caller entity's average; whether anything but the GRN's own receipt moved a lot it received into since (M4's ReverseGrn). |

**M2's questions** (`m2catalogue.api.InventoryLotQuery`, 22A section 6) are answered by `internal/availability/LotQuestionsForCatalogue` from the lots: `hasAnyLot(sku)` (a lot of the SKU exists anywhere) and `holdsLotOf(batch, scope)` (the caller's entity holds a lot of the batch). M2 asks them in its caller's scope about lots that scope need not read, so each is a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that answers yes or no and shows nothing, and since `V0007` (wave 2, RLS-12; the fifth case of `RLS_POLICY_TEMPLATE.md`) each answers an OWN caller only and takes the caller from `kernel.scope_entity()`: `inventory.sku_has_lot(sku)` and `inventory.caller_holds_lot_of(batch)`, both executable by `app_rw`. M2's stand-in `RegistrationLotQuery` and its test were deleted in the same pull request, as M2 planned; `InventoryQueriesPostgresIntegrationTest` proves CorrectBatch against real lots (a lot holder that did not register the batch corrects it, an entity without a lot is refused) and that a class other than OWN is answered nothing.

**M5's SQL contract for M2's correction trigger** (`V0002`, `V0007`; `docs/progress/deviations/2026-10-06-wave2-cross-tenant-functions.md` (3)): `inventory.entity_holds_lot_of(batch, entity) RETURNS boolean`, executable by the migrator alone (`EXECUTE` revoked from `app_rw` in `V0007`). `catalogue.batch_apply_correction` (m2catalogue `V0008`), a `SECURITY DEFINER` function of the migrator, calls it to refuse a correction by a caller that is neither the batch's owner, nor the Federation, nor a lot holder. This is the one recorded exception to the layering rule for SQL: a read-only call to a function M5 created for M2's question, never a table and never a write. Nothing else may call it; a change to its name or shape is a change to M2's migration too.

M3 and M4 have no stub to replace: M3 on main is still the scaffold, M4 is being built by another lane and calls `InventoryQueries` directly.

## M4's events (M5-03; 25A section 6.2)

The consumers read M4's payloads as JSON and import nothing of M4: M4 depends on M5's `query` package, so a dependency back would be a cycle, and the payload is the contract (the field names of M4's `GrnConfirmed`, `DeliveryNoteIssued` and `DeliveryNoteDispatched` records, frozen at M4-05). The consumer framework delivers each event once, in the OWN scope of its owner, with no user; each consumer hands a command to a handler, which audits and publishes.

| Event | Consumer | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `grn.confirmed.v1` | `GrnConsumer` (`m5.receipts`) → `ApplyGrnReceiptHandler` | The receiver's lots: RECEIPT of `received − damaged` GOOD and `damaged` DAMAGED per line, at the line's unit cost (the trade price), citing the GRN; the entity average re-averaged. Ownership passed when the receiver confirmed the GRN (AGENTS.md idea 2). Audit `STOCK_RECEIVED`; `stock.received.v1`. A GRN already applied is not applied again. |
| `delivery_note.issued.v1` | `DeliveryConsumer` (`m5.deliveries`) → `ReserveDeliveryHandler` | A pick list: per line, the seller's GOOD lots with free stock in FEFO order (only the named batch when the line names one), taken until the line is covered; a row with no lot for what is short. Availability subtracts the open picks. Audit `PICK_LIST_CREATED`; `pick_list.created.v1`. |
| `delivery_note.dispatched.v1` | `DeliveryConsumer` → `DispatchDeliveryHandler` | The picked units leave the seller's lots as TRANSFER_OUT citing the delivery note: in transit and still the seller's (doc 24 A-03) until the buyer's GRN; the pick list DISPATCHED, the reservation over. Audit `PICK_LIST_DISPATCHED`; `pick_list.dispatched.v1`. |
| `receipt.issued.v1` (a till's bundle, via the sync gateway) | `SaleConsumer` (`m5.sales`) → `ApplySaleHandler` | SALE per line with a quantity at the device's shop, at the entity average, citing the receipt, numbered in the device's sequence. The batch the till resolved, or when it is missing or unknown to M2 the shop's first lot of the item (FEFO). Never refused: an oversell goes negative (the ledger's `STOCK_LOT_NEGATIVE`, `lot.negative.v1`); a batch the shop held no lot of is `SALE_WITHOUT_LOT` (REVIEW, 25A DR-5); a line with no batch and no lot of its item is not posted and is `SALE_LINE_UNRESOLVED` (REVIEW). Audit `STOCK_SOLD`; `stock.sold.v1`. A receipt applied before is not applied again. Demo phase 3; the end-to-end proof with the till simulator is M6's `TillSaleEndToEndIntegrationTest`. Voids and refunds (SALE_REVERSAL) are deferred. |

Reads: `GET /v1/inventory/receipts/{grnId}` (`inv.stock.receive`) and `GET /v1/inventory/pick-lists/{deliveryNoteId}` (`whs.pick`). The consumer-applied commands carry those codes: the system runs them with no user, so nothing is checked, and `tools/check-permissions.mjs` needs every handler's code on an operation of the slice.

## The opening balance (M5-10 at demo scope; doc 25 section 4.7, flow 6.9)

`POST /v1/inventory/opening-balances` (`inv.opening.prepare`): the counted lines of a location (batch, condition, quantity, cost), DRAFT; refused where stock has moved or another is being prepared. `/{id}/sign` (`inv.opening.sign`, MFA): SIGNED_ENTITY. `/{id}/countersign` (`inv.opening.countersign`, MFA), by another person: the entity's OPB series is registered where missing, the OPB document issued (kernel issuance, ENTITY series; M5 owns the type through `OpeningBalanceDocumentType`), the OPENING_BALANCE movements posted citing it (lots and entity average seeded), POSTED. This is how the demo loader puts stock in.

**A line can carry its batch as counted (M5-12).** Instead of `batchId`, a line may give `skuId` with `batchNo`, `expiryDate` and `printedMrp` as the item needs them; the prepare registers the batch through M2's `BatchRegistration` in the same transaction and the entity's OWN scope, with the opening balance as its origin (`OPB-<first 8 of its id>` numbers a synthetic batch). M2's own guards answer for it (an active item, an expiry or MRP where required); the same item and number give back the batch already registered. This lets staff load opening stock from the web screen (`web/src/modules/m5inventory`), where no batch exists yet before the first GRN.

**The web screens (M5-12, demo scope; doc 30 section 5.5):** `/inventory` shows a location's lots (M1's location list; item names from M2) with FEFO rank, cost when the server sends it, and the availability of each item there; `/inventory/opening/new` prepares an opening balance; `/inventory/opening/:id` signs and countersigns it.

## Transfers (M5-09 at demo scope; 25A section 6.3, doc 25 flow 6.6)

An internal transfer moves stock between two locations of one entity (a society's warehouse to one of its shops). It never changes the owner and never moves the entity average.

| Step | Who | Writes (all at their own location) |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /v1/inventory/transfers` (`inv.transfer.issue`, ENTITY) → `IssueTransferHandler` | an entity-wide user of the entity | `transfer` and `transfer_line` rows at the **source**; TRANSFER_OUT per line at the source, at the entity average, which the line keeps as its cost. Audit `TRANSFER_ISSUED`; `transfer.issued.v1`. Status IN_TRANSIT. |
| `POST /v1/inventory/transfers/{id}/receive` (`shop.transfer.receive`, LOCATION) → `ReceiveTransferHandler` | the **destination's** session (a shop user), or an entity-wide user | one `transfer_receipt` row at the **destination**; TRANSFER_IN per line there at the line's cost. Audit `TRANSFER_RECEIVED`; `transfer.received.v1`. Status RECEIVED. |

**The own-location write rule (PR #148).** "Receiving a transfer must not update the source-side rows from the destination's session": nothing is updated at all. The transfer has two halves in two insert-only tables (`V0004`): the issue is the source's rows, the receipt is the destination's row, and the status is derived (RECEIVED when the receipt row exists). Each side reads the other half through an extra SELECT policy in the owner's OWN scope: `dest_read` on `transfer` and `transfer_line` (`to_location_id` is the session's location) and `source_read` on `transfer_receipt` (`from_location_id` is). The issuer is entity-wide because inv.transfer.issue is an ENTITY permission (25A section 3.1); a session held to the source cannot see the destination in M1 and is refused `m5.transfer.destination_invalid`. A session at the source reads the transfer but cannot receive it (`m5.transfer.not_destination`); a sibling shop does not see it.

Guards: `m5.scope.own_required`, `m5.location.not_in_scope`, `m5.transfer.same_location`, `m5.transfer.destination_invalid`, `m5.transfer.lines_required`, `m5.transfer.line_invalid`, `m5.batch.not_found`, `m5.transfer.insufficient_stock` (the source's GOOD lot of the batch holds less); `m5.transfer.not_found`, `m5.transfer.not_destination`, `m5.transfer.already_received`. Reads: `GET /v1/inventory/transfers?locationId=` (leaving or arriving, newest first) and `GET /v1/inventory/transfers/{id}` (`inv.stock.view`). Screen: `/inventory/transfers` (send from the chosen location, receive at it).

Deferred for the demo (`docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md`): the XFR document and its LOCATION series (the movements cite the transfer id), a separate dispatch step, cancel before receipt, a short receipt with its ADJ draft, `TransferRequestConsumer` (M4's lateral request), the till's `transfer.received.v1` bundle (`TransferReceiptHook`), `TransferOverdueJob` and `inventory.transfer_receipt_days`, the snapshot's expected transfers.

## Stock control: counts, write-offs, repacks (M5-11, M5-13 and the repack of 25A, back office)

`V0005` holds the tables; `internal/control/ControlPolicy` the shared rules; `query.StockControlQueries` the reads; `web/StockControlController` the slice's `StockControl` operations. The till's bundles (CountBundleHook, WriteOffDraftHook, RepackBundleHook) come with M6 (CR-30-1).

**Counts** (`internal/count`; doc 25 section 3.5, flow 6.3). `POST /v1/inventory/counts` (`inv.count.schedule`): a count of a location, FULL or by items (SKUS), SCHEDULED; one open count per location (`m5.count.already_open`). `/{id}/start` (`shop.count.record`): one `count_expectation` row per lot in scope with what the book holds (negative lots too), COUNTING. `/{id}/submit` (`shop.count.record`): every lot of the sheet counted or skipped with a reason; each line is measured against **the lot as it stands at submit**, which is the expectation at start plus every movement posted since (E-06, DR-8: the ledger is the only writer), so sales during the count are never a variance and nothing is frozen. The variance's value is at the entity average. Within tolerance (`inventory.count_tolerance_qty` 2 units, or `inventory.count_tolerance_pct` 1 % of the book, whichever is wider, F-10) it posts at once as COUNT_ADJUST citing the count; the rest waits: VARIANCE_REVIEW with its value and band (`inventory.approval_band1_limit` 25,000, `band2` 250,000, above: 3). `/{id}/approve` (`inv.adjust.approve`, MFA): not the submitter (`m5.adjustment.approver_is_requester`, and the SoD pair inv.adjust.request / inv.adjust.approve), within the approver's own limit (`max_value` of the permission on the approver's roles, failing closed since wave 2: see below; `m5.approval.limit_exceeded`); posts the recorded variances, CLOSED APPROVED. `/{id}/reject` (reason): CLOSED REJECTED, nothing more posted. Audit `COUNT_SCHEDULED`, `COUNT_STARTED`, `COUNT_SUBMITTED`, `ADJUSTMENT_APPROVED`, `ADJUSTMENT_REJECTED`; events `count.scheduled/started/submitted.v1`, `adjustment.approved/rejected.v1`.

**Negative lots** (doc 25 flow 6.2): `GET /v1/inventory/locations/{id}/negative-lots` lists the lots the tills oversold; `POST /v1/inventory/lots/{id}/acknowledge-negative` (`inv.count.schedule`, a note) sets `negative_acknowledged_at` (`STOCK_LOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `lot.acknowledged.v1`). The next count corrects the quantity. NegativeLotReviewJob is not built.

**Write-offs** (`internal/writeoff`; doc 25 section 3.8, flow 6.4). `POST /v1/inventory/write-offs` (`inv.writeoff.request`, a LOCATION permission, so a shop's own staff drafts at the shop): category (the nine of F-01), lines on lots that hold the quantity; the WOF document is saved as a kernel DRAFT so photographs can attach to it. `/{id}/photos`: the kernel presigns one upload against the draft (PENDING until its verifier sets COMPLETE). `/{id}/submit`: photographs required for the categories of `inventory.writeoff_photo_categories` (THEFT, SHRINKAGE_UNEXPLAINED) and at a single-staff location (size band S or `inventory.single_staff`); the location's WOF series registered where missing and the document issued; value at the entity average (DR-3) and band; REQUESTED. `/{id}/witness` (`inv.writeoff.witness`): not the requester, no photograph PENDING (a FAILED upload no longer blocks, wave 2); at a single-staff location the witness is remote, which needs `inventory.remote_witness_allowed` and the witness holding inv.writeoff.approve (DR-4); WITNESSED. `/{id}/approve` (`inv.writeoff.approve`, MFA): not the requester nor, since wave 2, the in-person witness (SoD pair inv.writeoff.request / inv.writeoff.approve), the value recomputed now within the approver's limit; WRITE_OFF per line at the entity average citing the WOF document; POSTED. `/{id}/reject` (reason): REJECTED, no stock moves, the document keeps its number.

**Repack** (`internal/repack`; doc 25 section 3.6, flow 6.5, doc 13 scenario 3). `POST /v1/inventory/recipes` (`inv.recipe.manage`): input and output items different, both in use, the output a REPACK_OUTPUT item of M2; `/{id}/retire`. `POST /v1/inventory/repacks` (`inv.repack.execute`): a quantity of one GOOD lot of the recipe's input is consumed (REPACK_CONSUME at the average), the output batch registered through M2's `BatchRegistration` with the input's expiry, and the packs made produced (REPACK_PRODUCE) at the consumed value divided by their number; expected output = the recipe scaled to the quantity taken, less its loss; yield variance = expected − actual. `/{id}/reverse` (`inv.repack.reverse`, reason): only while the output lot holds exactly what was made and nothing else moved it (`m5.repack.output_touched`); the output is taken back and the input lot restored at the cost it left with.

**Screens** (`web/src/modules/m5inventory`): `/inventory/counts` (schedule and start, the counts, the negative lots), `/inventory/counts/:id` (the stocktake sheet, then the adjustment voucher with Approve and Reject), `/inventory/write-offs` and `/inventory/write-offs/:id` (the damage and expiry register: draft, photographs through the shell's `AttachmentCapture`, submit, witness, approve, reject), `/inventory/repacks` (recipes, the production sheet, reversal).

## Wave 2 of the code review (6 October 2026: CR-25A-1)

Decided on the architect's delegation in `docs/progress/deviations/2026-10-06-wave2-expired-stock.md`, `2026-10-06-wave2-stock-approvals.md` and `2026-10-06-wave2-stock-movements.md`; built in the PR `fix(m5): expiry-aware stock, approval limits that fail closed, counting at the counted moment, the cost rules, locks and flags` (the cost rules, item (5), in their own PR).

- **Expired stock (M5-01).** A lot is expired when its expiry date is before the business date (`internal/control/BusinessDay`: Colombo's date from the application's clock, passed into every query, never SQL `current_date`); a lot expiring today sells today. Expired lots have no FEFO rank, are never available, never picked for a delivery note (`ConsumerStore.lockCandidates`) or a transfer request (`pickBatches`) and never price candidates (`inStockBatches`, which M3's AUTO_LOWEST reads); `balances` still shows them, `expired: true`, with an "Expired" badge on the stock screen. A delivery note to another entity also skips lots with less than `inventory.dispatch_min_shelf_life_days` (ENTITY, default 0) of life left. The sale fallback keeps expired lots (a sale is a fact) and flags `SALE_OF_EXPIRED` (REVIEW) when the batch is past its date on the receipt's business date. Expired stock leaves by a write-off (EXPIRED); the nightly `ExpiryScanJob` is a ticket of its own.
- **Approval limits fail closed (M5-09).** `ControlPolicy.requireWithinLimit`: the approver's authority is the highest `max_value` of the permission over their assignments at the entity; a grant without one approves up to `inventory.approval_band1_limit`; no grant is no authority (`m5.approval.limit_exceeded`, limit 0). A line is valued at the entity average, else its lot's cost (`internal/control/StockOnHand.unitValue`); a line of no cost routes the decision to band 2. The demo approver roles carry `max_value` (PR 11's block of `demo-users.demo.sql`).
- **Three people where three exist (M5-10, M5-11).** The approver may not be the in-person witness (`m5.writeoff.approver_is_witness`); a single-staff location's remote witness, who holds the approval, still may. The witness is accepted when no photograph is PENDING, each is COMPLETE or FAILED and, where photographs are required, one is COMPLETE (`m5.writeoff.photos_required` otherwise); FAILED ones are named in `WRITEOFF_WITNESSED`. The demo's write-off is witnessed by the society office (`m101-office`, granted `inv.writeoff.witness` in this PR's block of `demo-users.demo.sql`) and approved by the manager.
- **Locks in the ledger's order (M5-12).** Write-off approval and transfer issue lock the lots with `FOR UPDATE` in the ledger's `LotKey` order (`StockOnHand.lockLots`) before they check the quantity; `pickBatches` and the transfer issue subtract what open pick lists hold. Counts post a delta and lock nothing.
- **Counting at the counted moment, and by value (M5-13, M5-14).** `count_line.counted_at` (`V0008`), stamped by the web form as each quantity is typed: the line's book is the lot less the movements that occurred after it (by `occurred_at`), the moment held between the start of the count and now; a line without it keeps the submit-time rule. A variance auto-posts only within the quantity/percent tolerance **and** `inventory.count_tolerance_value` (Rs 1,000), and never a surplus on a lot whose book was zero or less; when the auto-posted total passes `inventory.count_autopost_value_cap` (Rs 10,000) every variance waits. Each cleared variance is listed in `COUNT_TOLERANCE_CLEARED` (REVIEW).
- **Repack yield (M5-18).** `inventory.repack_yield_tolerance_pct` (2 %): more packs than expected beyond it are refused (`m5.repack.yield_out_of_range`), fewer need a reason (`varianceReason`, `m5.repack.yield_reason_required`) and are `REPACK_YIELD_EXCEPTION` (REVIEW). A till's repack bundle, when it comes, flags and never refuses.
- **Requests, dispatch, receipts, sales (M5-02, M5-03, M5-05, M5-16).** An approved transfer request is filled once: each item sent short is `TRANSFER_REQUEST_SHORT` (REVIEW, wanted and sent) and counted in `transfer.issued.v1`'s additive `shortLines`; the shop raises a new request for the rest. A pick row short at the reservation is picked again at dispatch from the in-date stock there is then; what is still missing is `PICK_SHORT_DISPATCHED` (REVIEW), never posted negative. A transfer received by the person who issued it is `TRANSFER_SELF_RECEIVED` (REVIEW). A sale of an item the shop never held posts against the item's newest batch M2 knows (the lot goes negative, `SALE_WITHOUT_LOT`); only an item M2 lacks is `SALE_LINE_UNRESOLVED`.
- **Till facts without a hook (M5-04).** `TillFactNotAppliedConsumer` flags a till's `count.recorded`, `writeoff.requested`, `repack.executed` and `transfer.issued` bundles as `TILL_FACT_NOT_APPLIED` (REVIEW; `till_fact.not_applied.v1`) until each has its hook; central's own events of those types carry no device and are ignored.
- **Deferred, with the reason** (`2026-10-06-wave2-stock-movements.md` (7)): the `SaleReversalConsumer` (M5-04) ships with the till's voids and refunds, with M6-07 and M8's negative fact; M5-08 (one cost row per entity and item) waits for the 25A performance test; M5-15 (a short receipt and cancel of a transfer) before shops receive transfers at pilot; M5-19 (an opening balance's batch registered at countersign, the Federation countersigning) before the cutover exercise; the cost of oversold units at zero waits for M8's margin work. The half-rounding of a repack's output cost is accepted (the cost rules' section above).

## Deviations from 25A, with reasons

1. **`sku_id` on `stock_lot` and `stock_movement`.** The guide keys both on the batch alone, and every query of section 7 asks by SKU (balances, availability, the entity average): without the column each would join M2's `catalogue.batch` from this schema. The SKU is copied from M2's batch when the lot is created (doc 18 B-I3: a lot's batch is of the line's SKU) and never changes; so is the batch's expiry, onto the lot (`expiry_date`), which FEFO ranks by: a batch's expiry never changes either (a correction is a replacement batch, B-I8).
2. **`movement_sequence` is a table of its own.** 25A names `SequenceService` ("dense per (location, source)") and gives it no table; a counter row per (location, source), drawn in blocks under its row lock, keeps the numbers dense without gaps from rolled-back sequences. `SequenceService` is the block draw inside `LedgerService` (only a command handler may write).
3. **Property tests without jqwik.** jqwik is not in the build; `CostServiceTest` runs 5,000 generated cases on a fixed seed (25A section 9's count), and the interleaving property runs against PostgreSQL with concurrent postings.
4. **The average on an intake when the entity holds nothing or owes stock** is the intake's cost (above); 25A writes the formula only.
5. **Tables of later tickets are not in V0001**: recipes, the policy tables, count and expiry tasks, pick lists, the loss categories and the document extensions come with the tickets that use them, each in a migration of its own, so no table exists that no code writes.
6. **Demo scope of M5-04**: the slice has the two reads the demo needs (balances, availability); the stock card (`/skus/{id}/movements`), the availability cache of 30 seconds and the PARTY callers' "live or end of day" mode (25A section 7) are deferred (`docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md`, "Deferred after the demo"). M4 reads availability in the seller's own scope, which needs none of them. **The stock card came on 28 Sep** (branch `feat/m5-stock-card`) as `GET /v1/inventory/locations/{locationId}/movements?skuId=` (`inv.stock.view`, under RLS): the movements of one SKU at one location in ledger order (received, source, sequence), each with `balanceAfter`, the running quantity of the SKU at the location over all lots and conditions, summed by the database; the unit cost at the movement only for an owner's user, like the balances. It is keyed by location rather than 25A's `/skus/{id}/movements` because a stock card is a location's book of an item. No paging or date window yet (see Deferred after the demo). The web screen is `/inventory/locations/:locationId/skus/:skuId`, linked from each item of the stock position.
7. **Pick list lines are rows, not jsonb** (25A section 3 keeps them as jsonb): availability sums the open picks per location and SKU.
8. **The delivery note names no dispatching warehouse** (M4's `DeliveryNoteIssued` carries drops and lines only): the pick list takes each line from the seller's GOOD lots across all its locations in FEFO order. With one warehouse per seller in the demo this is the warehouse; M4 adding a `fromLocationId` would narrow it.
9. **Dispatch posts TRANSFER_OUT at the seller**, the one existing movement type for stock leaving a location in transit; 25A names only the reservation at issue. The goods stay the seller's in transit (doc 24 A-03) and the GRN receives them at the buyer. The entity quantity drops at dispatch.
10. **Quantities on a delivery note are taken in the item's base unit**; the conversion of a line's unit (M2) is deferred.
11. **The opening balance's draft and signatures live in `inventory.opening_balance`**, and the OPB document is issued on countersign (the moment 25A section 4.7 issues it), instead of the `doc_opening_balance` extension of a stored draft document. The "location ONBOARDING" guard is not applied (the demo loads active locations); the "no movements at the location" guard is. The countersignature is given in the owning entity's scope by a person other than the signer; a Federation officer countersigning from the Federation's own scope is deferred.
12. **New events** not in doc 25 section 5.3: `stock.received.v1`, `pick_list.created.v1`, `pick_list.dispatched.v1`, `opening_balance.changed.v1` (prepared and signed); each handler publishes one, as every handler must.
13. **A transfer is two insert-only halves, not one XFR document row** (`V0004`): 25A extends an XFR document (`doc_transfer`) with dispatch and receipt columns on one row, which the destination would update, against the own-location write rule of PR #148. The issue (source) and the receipt (destination) are rows of their own, the status is derived, and the movements cite the transfer id until the XFR document is issued (deferred). Issue and dispatch are one step.
14. **No CNT, ADJ or RPK document yet** (accepted on the architect’s delegation, 29 Sep): a count and its adjustment are one `count_task` with its expectation and lines (V0005), a repack is a `repack` row and its reversal a `repack_reversal` row; their movements cite the task or repack id, as the transfers cite theirs (13). The WOF document **is** issued (LOCATION series, registered at the location where missing), because photographs attach to a kernel document. The adjustment lives on the count (`/counts/{id}/approve|reject`), not on `/adjustments`.
15. **One tolerance and two band limits per entity** (configuration items, `ControlPolicy`) instead of the per-SKU and per-tag `variance_threshold` and `loss_tolerance` tables; the loss categories are the fixed list of doc 25 F-01 in the API, not a `loss_category` table; photographs are required per `inventory.writeoff_photo_categories` and at single-staff locations.
16. **Schedule and start are two commands, the web form runs them together**; scopes FULL and SKUS only (TAGS and EXPIRY come with the expiry job); one open count per location. The expectation at submit is the lot as it stands then (the start expectation plus every movement since, E-06), so no movement is read back. Since wave 2 a line that carries `counted_at` is measured against the lot as it stood then (M5-13).
17. **PlanRepack and ExecuteRepack are one step; one input lot per repack; the quantity taken is the operator’s** and the expected output scales the recipe to it. The reversal restores the input at the unit cost it left with and cites the repack; no REVERSES link.
18. **A remote witness is a witness at a single-staff location** (size band S or `inventory.single_staff`), who must hold inv.writeoff.approve where `inventory.remote_witness_allowed`; the remote witness may also approve (flow 6.4), the requester never; since wave 2 the in-person witness may not (M5-10).
19. **The approver’s limit** is the highest `max_value` of the permission on the approver’s roles at the entity (M1’s `SecurityQueries`); since wave 2 a grant without limits approves band 1 only and no grant is no authority (M5-09).
20. **ClaimReturn consumes `claim.return_dispatched.v1`, not `claim.approved.v1`** (M4-06, 29 Sep; 25A section 6.2): the approval is the seller’s event, delivered in the seller’s scope, which cannot write the buyer’s lots; the buyer’s own act of sending the goods back is (m4trading README, deviation 27). `ClaimReturnConsumer` hands `ApplyClaimReturn` to its handler, which posts RETURN_TO_SELLER (GOOD, at the entity average) citing the claim, once; STOCK_RETURNED_TO_SELLER, `stock.returned_to_seller.v1`.
21. **An approved transfer request is issued by `TransferRequestConsumer`** (M4-10; 25A section 6.2, “transfer_request.approved.v1: IssueTransfer”): it picks the source’s GOOD lots first-expiry-first (`pickBatches`) for each requested item and hands IssueTransfer, now carrying `transferRequestId`, to the issue handler; the transfer row names the request (V0006, one transfer per request, a redelivery finds it) and `transfer.issued.v1` carries it. Stock sold since the approval is sent as far as it goes; with nothing left no transfer is issued and the request is flagged `TRANSFER_REQUEST_SHORT` (REVIEW) with `transfer_request.unfilled.v1` (`RecordTransferRequestUnfilledHandler`; wave 3, M1M2M3M5-17), where it used to fail into the dead letter queue. `InventoryQueries.transferOfRequest` answers M4.
