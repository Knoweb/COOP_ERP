Cooperative Retail and Distribution System
04  -  Process Flows
Baseline requirements package  --  document 4 of 10
Twelve flows, given as numbered actor steps rather than drawn BPMN diagrams, so this stays exact and reviewable in text. Each maps directly onto the document state machines in Document 3 and can be converted to formal BPMN swimlane diagrams at design time without changing the logic.Flow 1: Inter-entity trade cycle
Actors: Buying entity, selling entity
 1. Buyer raises an Order against a seller it holds a trading relationship with.
 2. Seller accepts (in full or part, per the allocation rule if stock is short) and sets an indicative ETA.
 3. Order locks for edit/cancel at the configured window before ETA.
 4. Seller dispatches; a Delivery note is created (one or more drops).
 5. Receiving party signs on delivery; a GRN is raised, ownership transfers.
 6. If received quantity differs from dispatched, a Discrepancy is raised and investigated.
 7. Seller issues an Invoice against the closed GRN(s).
 8. Buyer's credit headroom decreases by the invoiced (or, if configured, committed) exposure.
 9. Seller posts a Payment receipt when payment is received; headroom is released.

Flow 2: Direct-drop delivery to shops
Actors: MPCS (bill-to), Shop (ship-to), Distributor/Fed (seller)
 1. MPCS raises the order; seller accepts against the MPCS's credit relationship.
 2. Seller's vehicle plans a multi-shop route; one Delivery note references several drop points.
 3. At each shop, shop staff sign the GRN on behalf of the owning MPCS.
 4. Ownership transfers to the MPCS at each shop's GRN signature, stock lands directly at that shop location.
 5. MPCS's aggregate inventory (store + all shops) updates accordingly; invoice is raised once against the MPCS.

Flow 3: Repack (bulk to retail)
Actors: Warehouse/shop staff with repack permission
 1. Staff select a predefined repack recipe (input SKU + quantity --> output SKU + quantity, expected yield).
 2. Input batch(es) are consumed from stock.
 3. Output batch is created, inheriting traceability to the input batch(es).
 4. Actual yield is compared to the recipe's expected yield; material variance is flagged for review.
 5. If a mistake is found and output stock is still unsold, a permission-gated Reversal restores the input and removes the output.

Flow 4: Write-off (loss, damage, expiry disposal)
Actors: Requesting staff, witness, approving role
 1. Staff identify stock to be written off and select a loss category (damaged / expired / theft / shrinkage / other, per the F-01 category list).
 2. A write-off request is raised, capturing photographic evidence where practical.
 3. A different person witnesses physical disposal (segregation of duties: requester != approver).
 4. A permitted role approves; if raised offline, this step is deferred until the site is online.
 5. Stock is removed from the ledger; the event is written to the immutable audit trail.

Flow 5: GRN discrepancy handling
Actors: Receiving staff, dispatching entity, federation (if unresolved)
 1. Receiving staff record what was actually received, not what was on the dispatch note.
 2. A Discrepancy record is raised automatically where actual != dispatched.
 3. The dispatching entity is notified and investigates within an agreed time limit.
 4. Resolution (correction, replacement, credit note) is agreed bilaterally; the system records the outcome.
 5. If unresolved between the two parties, federation may be drawn in as a fall-back arbiter.

Flow 6: Lateral transfer between shops
Actors: Shop (short), MPCS, Shop (source)
 1. A shop identifies a stock shortage and raises a request to its MPCS (shops cannot see sibling shop stock directly).
 2. MPCS reviews stock availability across all its shops and its central store.
 3. If another shop or the store can cover it, MPCS approves a lateral Stock transfer (intra-entity, at cost, no invoice).
 4. If not, MPCS raises an Order upward to its distributor (or federation, once that channel is open).

Flow 7: Customer credit sale and repayment
Actors: Cashier, tagged/credit customer, MPCS accounts role
 1. At billing, cashier tags the sale to a registered customer (looked up by phone; internal ID is the true key).
 2. Sale posts against the customer's entity-scoped account; balance increases.
 3. System checks the account's credit limit; a warning banner shows if headroom is marginal or exceeded  --  the sale is never blocked on this basis.
 4. Customer later makes a repayment; MPCS accounts role posts it against the account.
 5. Ageing and statement reports are available to the MPCS for that customer.

Flow 8: Stocktake / cycle count
Actors: Counting staff, approving role
 1. A count task is generated  --  either scheduled cycle counting or a full stocktake, or system-generated when a batch enters its expiry warning window.
 2. Staff count physical stock; the system compares to the ledger position.
 3. Variance is escalated for approval if it exceeds the configured threshold for that SKU or SKU group.
 4. Approved adjustment posts to the stock ledger, fully audit-logged.

Flow 9: Offline sale and synchronisation
Actors: Cashier, till (local cache), central system
 1. Till operates from its local cache (catalogue, prices, promotions, tax categories, roles/permissions) with no live connection.
 2. Sale completes locally: price lookup, stock deduction, receipt printed with a till-position series number.
 3. Sale event is queued locally, append-only, immutable.
 4. On reconnection, queued events upload to central; central deduplicates by event ID.
 5. Central checks each till-position series for gaps  --  a missing number signals an event that has not yet arrived, and is flagged for follow-up.

Flow 10: Price / catalogue propagation
Actors: MPCS (pricing role), federation (SKU steward), shop tills
 1. MPCS sets or updates its retail price list, or federation updates the shared SKU catalogue.
 2. Change is validated against the MRP ceiling for any regulated item.
 3. Change propagates to all of that MPCS's (or federation's) affected shop till caches on next sync.
 4. A shop offline at the time of change continues selling at its last-cached price until it reconnects.

Flow 11: Shop onboarding
Actors: MPCS, field deployment team, new shop staff
 1. Shop location is created under its owning MPCS or distributor; till positions are provisioned and register their numbering series.
 2. Hardware is installed; local cache is seeded with the current catalogue, prices and permissions.
 3. Opening stock count is performed and entered as an opening-balance stock position.
 4. Any pre-existing customer/khata balances for that shop are entered as signed, two-party opening-balance documents.
 5. Staff training is delivered against the standard playbook; the shop goes live.

Flow 12: Claim and return to source
Actors: Receiving entity/shop, responsible entity
 1. Damage, shortage or quality issue is identified after GRN  --  a Claim is raised with photographic evidence.
 2. Responsible entity inspects (per the claim's terms, bilaterally agreed  --  the system tracks status only).
 3. If approved, a Credit note is issued against the original invoice.
 4. If goods are physically returned, a return movement records the reverse stock and ownership transfer.
