## m9integration/internal/journal (4)
- **M9-01 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/m9integration/internal/journal/JournalPostingsConsumer.java:59** [low, was medium] [data integrity] A posting's business date is the Colombo day of the event's `occurredAt` (when it was published), not the document's own business date, which 29A §6.2 uses (`d.businessDate`). (PR #229)
  Scenario: A cheque received on 30 September is recorded on 2 October with `receivedOn=2026-09-30`, which `RecordPaymentReceiptHandler:121` allows. The PRC posting gets `business_date=2026-10-02`. The September export leaves out a receipt that the receipt document, the statement and M8 all date in September, and the October export includes it. The same thing happens to any document whose date is not the publish instant: a consumer redelivery does not change `occurredAt`, but a demo load at historical time or a later tax-point rule does. 29A §6.2 says the date is `d.businessDate`. Fix: M4 puts the document's business date (tax point date, `receivedOn`, issue date) on `journal.postings_ready.v1`, and M9 records that date. `occurredAt` should be used only when the payload carries no date.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED, narrowed; severity lowered to low.** The headline scenario is not a defect against the design. 29A §6.2 selects `documents.issuedIn(period)` and dates the line `d.businessDate`, which is `kernel.document.business_date`. For an entity-level INV, CN, DN2 or PRC, `IssuanceProtocol.businessDateFor` (lines 251-261, CR-19A-8) sets that to the Colombo calendar date of the issue instant. The PRC recorded on 2 October with `receivedOn=30 Sep` therefore has document business date 2 October under the design as well. Doc 29 §6.3 ("a late document … not edited into the export; a supplement") agrees. Dating receipts by `receivedOn` instead would be a design change, not a fix. What does hold: M9 derives the date from `occurredAt` instead of carrying `d.businessDate`. The two match for every current publisher, because all are entity-level and issue and publish happen in one transaction. They differ in two cases. (a) An issue just before midnight whose outbox `occurredAt` falls after midnight. (b) Location-dated documents, whose date is `businessDate.currentHeld(location)` and can lag the calendar while a day close is pending. M5's WOF/ADJ are one example ("the consumer takes them unchanged", README) and the BUYER-side GRN of M9-02 is another.
  Suggested fix scope: code only (M4 adds `businessDate` to the `JournalPostingsReady` payload, and M9 reads it, falling back to `occurredAt`). No migration. A design decision is needed only if receipts should be dated by `receivedOn` rather than by issue date (an accounting-policy question for the architect).
- **M9-02 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/m9integration/internal/journal/RecordJournalPostingsHandler.java:101** [medium] [data integrity] Every posting is filed under the event owner's entity, whatever its `side`. M4 publishes only the SELLER side, so the buyer's books (payable, VAT input, GRN accrual) never reach any export. (PR #229)
  Scenario: M4's posting map seeds `INV GOODS BUYER` (GRN_ACCRUAL/PAYABLE, VAT_INPUT/PAYABLE) and `GRN GOODS BUYER`, but every `postings.postings(...)` call in M4 passes "SELLER". An MPCS that buys from its distributor and asks for its journal gets `m9.journal.nothing_to_export` for its purchases, so its accounting package never sees its payables. If M4 starts sending BUYER rows on the seller's event, the consumer stores them with `owner_entity_id = seller`, and they land in the seller's journal. Fix: route each posting to the entity its side names (BUYER goes to the counterparty, which M4 must put on the event). That needs a system-scoped insert for the counterparty, or a second event that M4 publishes in the buyer's scope. Until then, list the gap as a deviation in the README.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED by reading.** `seed/m4trading/posting-map.yaml:15-17` has the three BUYER rows. All six `postings.postings(...)` calls (IssueInvoice:235, IssueCreditNote:133, ApproveClaim:182, SettleDiscrepancy:210, RecordPaymentReceipt:195, RecordChequeOutcome:209) pass "SELLER". `RecordJournalPostingsHandler:101` writes `scope.entityId()` regardless of `line.side()`, and the handler accepts BUYER as well-formed (line 130). Doc 29 §3.3 and §6.2 require "GRN and INV (buyer)". The M9 README's deviation list does not mention the missing buyer side; it mentions only M5/M7.
  Suggested fix scope: a design decision is needed on how the buyer's postings reach the buyer's scope (M4 publishes a second event in the buyer's scope, or M9 inserts under a system scope for the named counterparty). The code change is in M4 and M9. No M9 schema migration (`side` and `owner_entity_id` already exist).
- **M9-03 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/m9integration/internal/journal/RequestJournalExportHandler.java:96** [medium] [data integrity] An export can cover a day that has not ended, and nothing reports a posting that arrives later dated in a period already exported. Such a posting stays out of every later export unless someone asks again for that exact old period. (PR #229)
  Scenario: At 15:00 on 30 September the accounts officer exports 1–30 September. Invoices issued from 15:00 to 23:59 on 30 September, or a September event that a dead-lettered consumer replays in October, get September business dates. The 1–31 October export filters `business_date between` 1 October and 31 October and skips them. The officer sees no warning, and the September journal in the accounting package stays short. The README defers the day-close guard and the provisional flag, but has no compensating check. Fix: refuse a `periodTo` on or after the current business date until the M8 freshness query exists, or mark such an export provisional. Have every export, or the list screen, report "postings dated before periodFrom not yet exported" (the `pending` query over open periods).
  **Verification: CONFIRMED by reading.** Doc 29 §4.2 sets the guard "period closed (all locations' day-close past period end) or explicit 'provisional' flag", and §6.2 says a provisional export "is marked PROVISIONAL in the file and superseded later". 29A §5 says the same. The handler has neither: it always inserts `provisional=false` (line 151), and the guard is a deliberate, documented deviation (README deviation 2, "wait for M8's freshness query"). The supplement behaviour, where a re-export of the same period takes only what arrived since, matches doc 29 §6.3. So the remaining defect is an open period exported as final with no mark. It is worse than the reviewer stated, because the web form's default period ends today (`integrationView.ts:22-25`, `defaultExportPeriod` = first day of the month two months back to today), so the default action exports the current, open day. Partial mitigation: the form shows the pending postings for whatever period is entered (`JournalExportsPage.tsx:34`), so re-entering September shows the late postings. Nothing flags them when another period is entered.
  Suggested fix scope: code only. The `journal_export.provisional` column already exists (V0001:47). An interim rule is needed until M8's freshness query exists: either `periodTo >= today` is provisional, or it is refused. That is a small design decision; the doc already prefers the provisional flag.
- **M9-05 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/m9integration/internal/queries/IntegrationQueriesImpl.java:116** [low] [maintainability] The delivered file is rebuilt from `journal_line` by the current `JournalFile.csv` code at each download. The stored hash therefore depends on that code, and no format version is stored. (PR #229)
  Scenario: A later change to the CSV, such as a new column, a different escaping rule in `text()`, or a BOM, changes the bytes. Every past export then reconciles `matchesRecordedHash=false`, and a re-download no longer matches the file the accounting package imported. Fix: store the generated file (object storage, as 29A says, or a bytea), or record a `format_version` and keep each version's writer.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED by reading.** README deviation 2 records "built from the lines on download rather than stored in object storage; the hash proves it unchanged". That claim holds only while `JournalFile.csv` never changes, and nothing records which writer produced the hash. This is a latent risk, with no wrong outcome today.
  Suggested fix scope: a migration is needed for either option (a `format_version` column on `journal_export`, or a stored-file column or object key). Choosing between them is a small design decision; 29A already says object storage.

## db/migration/m9integration (1)
- **M9-06 backend/app/src/main/resources/db/migration/m9integration/V0003__contacts_own_rls_and_resolver.sql:24-40** (the original citation of line 232 was wrong: the file has 40 lines) [low, was medium] [security] `integration.notification_recipients(p_entity, p_role)` is SECURITY DEFINER and EXECUTE is granted to `app_rw`, with no check against the caller's scope. Any `app_rw` session can therefore read every entity's contact addresses, and the own-entity RLS added in the same migration does not hold. (PR #229)
  Scenario: In a seller's OWN scope (as the test `aSellerCannotReadTheBuyersContactsYetItsInvoiceStillReachesThem` sets up), `select * from integration.notification_recipients('<any entity id>', 'ACCOUNTS')` returns that entity's e-mail addresses and phone numbers in clear text. Entity ids appear in many responses, and the role codes are a short fixed set (ACCOUNTS, MANAGER), so a single SQL injection, a mis-scoped query, or a careless future caller can enumerate the whole directory. The `fed_view` policy on the table also lets any FEDERATION_VIEW session read all contacts, against "own-entity only". Fix: inside the function, return rows only when `p_entity = kernel.scope_entity()` or `p_entity` is a counterparty of the scope entity (an active trading relationship in M1 or M4). Better still, take the event id and derive the parties from the outbox row. Drop `fed_view` on `notification_contact`, or restrict it to a named permission.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED by reading; severity lowered to low (defence in depth, no reachable path today).** The function body (lines 30-37) has no reference to `kernel.scope_entity()`, `scope_class()` or any relationship. `REVOKE ALL … FROM PUBLIC` (line 39) holds, so PUBLIC cannot call it, but `GRANT EXECUTE … TO app_rw` (line 40) means every application session can. An app_rw session in any scope (OWN at an unrelated entity, a location scope, FEDERATION_VIEW or EXTERNAL_TIMEBOXED) gets the ACTIVE `channel, address, language` of any entity for any role code, given its UUID. It cannot reach inactive contacts, other columns, or a listing of all entities. Today the only caller is `ContactAudience.resolve`, which passes the event's owner or counterparty as bound parameters, and no M9 endpoint reads contacts. An outside user would therefore need a second defect (SQL injection, or a future caller passing a user-supplied entity id). That is why the severity is low, even though the function does bypass, at the database layer, the own-entity rule that V0003 states for itself. On `fed_view` (line 16): it is the standard four-class template policy, and no API exposes contacts, so FEDERATION_VIEW can read them only through SQL. It does contradict V0003's own comment ("an entity reads and writes its own contacts only"), and the README does not record it. The reproduction test (scratch, not run because Docker was stopped) inserts a contact for an entity, shows that a direct `select` from an unrelated OWN scope returns 0 rows, and shows that the resolver returns the address from that same scope.
  Suggested fix scope: a migration (`CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION` with a scope check; drop or narrow `fed_view`). A design decision is needed on what "counterparty" means inside the function. A relationship lookup would read M1 or M4 tables from M9's function, against module boundaries. The alternatives are to verify through the outbox row (take the event id and require `owner = scope_entity()` with `p_entity` among the event's parties), or to require `p_entity = scope_entity()` and have the dispatcher resolve in a system scope.

## kernel/internal/notification (5)
- **M9-07 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/kernel/internal/notification/NotificationLog.java:66** [medium] [privacy] The recipient hash is an unsalted SHA-256 of `channel:address`, and M9 returns its first 12 hex characters to the event owner's log screen. A 10-digit Sri Lankan phone number can be recovered by brute force, so the seller learns the buyer's private ACCOUNTS number, which V0003 hides. (hash from PR #99; exposed by PR #229 at IntegrationQueriesImpl.java:242)
  Scenario: A distributor user with `int.notify.view` opens `/v1/integration/notifications/log`. The cheque-bounced SMS rows are owned by the distributor (the event owner) and are addressed to the MPCS's ACCOUNTS phone. Hashing `SMS:07XXXXXXXX` over the roughly 10^8 mobile numbers takes seconds and matches the 48-bit prefix, which reveals the number. Fix: use a keyed HMAC, with the key held like `pending-key`, for `recipient_hash`. Have the log endpoint show no hash material for recipients outside the caller's entity, or a per-row opaque label.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED (brute force reproduced).** `NotificationLog.hash` (lines 66-73) is plain SHA-256 over `channel + ":" + recipient.strip()`, with no key or salt. `NotificationService:141` stores it on the row owned by `ctx.entityId()`, which is the event owner, so the seller owns rows addressed to the buyer. `IntegrationQueriesImpl:241` returns `substring(0, 12)` (48 bits), and `NotificationsPage.tsx:204` renders it in the "recipient" column. In the demo seed, `int.notify.view` is held by the "Demo: accounts" role of the Federation, the distributor and the MPCS (`demo-users.demo.sql:261-264`) and by the Federation administrator (306). Rows are under `own_read` plus the template `fed_view` (kernel V0054:43-52). Measured: a single-threaded Node script on this machine found a 070-prefixed number from its 12-hex prefix after 4.6 million tries in 6.5 s. The whole 07X mobile space (10^8) takes about 2-3 minutes, and a false match on 48 bits among 10^8 candidates is negligible. A guessable e-mail address (`accounts@<society domain>`) falls to a dictionary. Doc 29 §8 and doc 19 §7 ask for "recipients hashed" and "phone numbers are hashed in the log", which an unkeyed hash of a low-entropy value does not achieve.
  Suggested fix scope: code plus a small design decision (an HMAC key held like the `pending-key` seal key, and whether the screen shows any hash for another entity's recipients). No schema migration: `recipient_hash` stays text. Existing rows keep their old hashes, which affects only the one-hour de-duplication and the unique (rule, event, recipient_hash) key across the cut-over. The endpoint-only mitigation (no hash for counterparty recipients) can ship alone.
- **M9-08 backend/app/src/main/java/lk/coopfed/knoweb/kernel/internal/notification/NotificationSuppression.java:62** [medium] [correctness] Quiet hours suppress a notification for good, at queue time and before each retry, when doc 29 §6.4 says "quiet hours: deferred to 07:00". With the seeded default `22:00-07:00`, M9's cheque-bounced SMS is lost whenever a bounce is recorded in the evening. (suppression from #99/#127; relied on by #229's SMS rule)
  Scenario: At 22:15 the distributor records a bounced cheque. The SMS row is SUPPRESSED with QUIET_HOURS, its hold is never written or is cleared, and the MPCS accounts desk never gets the SMS (only the e-mail). The same happens to an SMS whose first attempt failed at 21:59: its retry at 22:00 is SUPPRESSED. Quiet hours are also read in the event owner's scope (the seller's), not the recipient's, against doc 19's "per recipient quiet hours". Fix: during quiet hours, leave the row QUEUED with `next_attempt_at` set to the end of the window, keep the hold, and exclude that wait from the 24-hour age limit. Read the window for the recipient's entity.
  **Verification: CONFIRMED (reproduced by an existing test).** Doc 29 §6.4 reads, in its exceptions column, "quiet hours: deferred to 07:00". Doc 19 §7 reads "Per recipient quiet hours (22:00–07:00 default for SMS)". 19A §10 only says "quiet hours … checked before send", and its test list says "quiet-hours suppression". The kernel followed the 19A wording, so the design documents conflict. In code: `NotificationService:150-153` marks the row SUPPRESSED before `hold` is written. The retry path (`:211-215`) marks it SUPPRESSED and clears the hold. A suppressed row is never claimed again, so the message is gone. The existing test `NotificationsPostgresIntegrationTest.theKillSwitchAndTheQuietHoursSuppress` (lines 204-236) asserts exactly this: nothing sent, `suppressed_reason = QUIET_HOURS`. The seeded default is `notification.sms.quiet_hours = "22:00-07:00"`, ENTITY scope (`seed/kernel/config-items.yaml:178-185`). It is read with `ctx`/`owner`, the event owner's scope. `NotificationDeliveryPostgresIntegrationTest.arrange` even clears the window "for the seller" so that the buyer's SMS goes out. So the window is the sender's, not the recipient's.
  Suggested fix scope: a design decision is needed first. Doc 29 §6.4 ("deferred") and doc 19's "per recipient" conflict with 19A's "suppression" test, and the architect should state which governs and whose window applies, given that M1 keeps no per-person settings. The code change is in the kernel (`NotificationSuppression` returns a "defer until" instant, and `NotificationService` keeps the hold and sets `next_attempt_at`). No migration (`next_attempt_at` exists, kernel V0054:29). The existing kernel test must be changed with it.

## Covered, no findings
- Journal balance: every line debits one role and credits another with the same amount, and the role-by-role check runs (RequestJournalExportHandler). Amounts are numeric(14,2), totals numeric(16,2), `setScale(2, UNNECESSARY)` in the CSV, and decimals are parsed from text, never from a double.
- Exported once: the unique `journal_line.posting_id`, the per-entity `pg_advisory_xact_lock`, and the `not exists` filter under RLS. Concurrent exports serialize, and a second export finds nothing or only the supplement. Redelivery of the same postings is stopped by unique (document_id, seq) plus the count check.
- Reversals: the cheque-bounce reversal is its own PRC document with the REVERSAL map (roles swapped, positive amount). The consumer's negative-amount swap is consistent with that and does not double-swap.
- The hash is over the UTF-8 bytes that the download returns (`text/csv; charset=UTF-8`, same writer, lines ordered by seq). Reconciliation recomputes totals, by-role sums and the hash.
- CSV: formula-prefix neutralisation and RFC 4180 quoting in `JournalFile.text`.
- RLS on journal_posting, journal_export and journal_line (four-class template, insert-only grants). The exports, lines, file and reconciliation endpoints answer 404 across entities. The x-permission codes match `@CommandHandler`.
- SetNotificationRuleStatusHandler: Federation-only guard, the `FOR UPDATE` lock, the column-only UPDATE grant plus RLS policy, then audit, event, and the dispatcher cache cleared through `CacheFanoutListener` on every instance (#132). Rule cache TTL from the register.
- Renderer: plain-text mail, so there is no HTML injection. Placeholders are ICU arguments, never parsed as pattern. Language picked from the contact, falling back to English for both subject and body. `-u-nu-latn` for Latin digits. "Rs" in all three languages (V0004). Dates shown as dd/MM/yyyy. The tender and the approval band shown as words through select/plural (V0005, #241).
- Sealed holds (#145): AES-256-GCM with a fresh nonce and the notification id as AAD, cleared on SENT, SUPPRESSED and FAILED. No default key outside a development issuer, and deploy bootstrap generates one. The clear columns were dropped in V0063.
- Dispatcher idempotency: unique (rule, event, recipient_hash) with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. Per-recipient `ProblemException` does not roll back earlier recipients. Sends happen after commit, with claim-based retries.
- LogSmsGateway logs only the id and the length. The SMTP failure text is scrubbed. M9 events and audit rows carry no phone, address or token. The dev contacts seed uses `.test` addresses and placeholder numbers.
- i18n: every `m9.*` problem id has en/si/ta entries. Tests use fixed instants, not "today" (the quiet-hours test clears the window explicitly).
- #237 (demo doc): no secrets beyond the documented demo password, which belongs to the deploy area.
