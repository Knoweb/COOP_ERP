1. Purpose, scope and what this document decides

This is the first design artefact of Stage 1. It fixes the shape of the system at the level everything else depends
on: which parts exist, what each owns, how they may depend on one another, how business events flow, and
where the outside world connects. It stops short of table-level detail (doc 18), the internals of the cross-cutting
services (doc 19), provider-specific infrastructure (doc 14, doc 35) and screens (doc 30).

8. Event stream and queue

The event stream is the durable, replayable record of business facts; the work queue is a delivery mechanism
for jobs. The design keeps them apart (doc 19 section 6 names the four kinds of event and their persistence).

8.1 Where events live

Decision (Proposed ­ ADR-30). The system of record for domain events is an append-only outbox archive in
PostgreSQL written in the same transaction as the business change; a relay publishes rows to the broker; the
broker holds nothing that cannot be re-derived from the archive. Central write volume is on the order of a
thousand events a second at peak and a few thousand during reconnection bursts (doc 14 section 6), which
PostgreSQL handles; replay and audit come free; there is one fewer stateful system; the broker is replaceable.
The archive is partitioned by month and retained indefinitely.

8.2 Envelope

One envelope for every event, wherever it is created (doc 19 section 6.1): event id (UUIDv7), versioned dotted
type, occurred and received times, source (central or device), dense per-source sequence, owning entity and
location, aggregate type and id, correlation and causation ids, actor, and a small payload. The pair (source,
source sequence) is the idempotency key for ingestion and the basis of gap detection.

8.3 Delivery guarantees and consumer rules

· At-least-once delivery. Every consumer is idempotent by event id; the kernel supplies a per-consumer inbox
   table.

· Ordering per source, not globally. Events from one till, or from central, apply in sequence; cross-source
   order is by received time with freshness stamps.

· Consumers are registered in code, reviewed, and listed in the module catalogue; the dependency graph
   stays visible.

· Replay is first-class for read models; layer 2 modules never replay: their state is corrected by compensating
   documents, never by re-processing history.

· Poison events go to a dead-letter queue with an alert after three attempts and never block a source's
   subsequent events.

8.5 Reference-data distribution to tills is not the event stream

Decision (Proposed ­ ADR-34). Tills do not subscribe to domain events. They receive versioned snapshots
and deltas assembled per shop by the edge sync gateway: catalogue with thumbnails for the shop's assortment,
prices, rules, control prices, tax, lot lists, credit customers and balances, permissions, message catalogue. Each
snapshot carries a version and an apply_from business date; the till applies it atomically so a bill is never
computed against a half-applied price list. Read-only at the till (P5).
