31 – Application Update and Distribution

How software reaches and stays current on up to 15,000 kiosk-locked till terminals
across all 25 districts with no on-site IT, on poor links, without stopping trade: device
provisioning, the update agent, signed releases, ring rollout, health gates, rollback, the
version floor, and the back-office deployment that has to stay compatible with all of it.

Why this is a design problem and not an operations chore. A terminal in a shop with one cashier and no IT will
never have an update installed by hand. Every update must arrive by itself, install itself when the shop is closed, prove
it is healthy, and undo itself if it is not – and the central system must keep serving the versions that have not yet
arrived. Get this wrong and the fleet fragments into versions nobody can support (Q-10).

3. Android developer verification: what it means for a private till
app

Google is introducing a requirement that applications installed on certified Android devices come from
identity-verified developers, with enforcement beginning in September 2026 in a first group of countries and
expanding globally in 2027. The published guidance, as of August 2026, is that enterprise deployment is exempt
when the application is installed through an EMM (enterprise mobility management) device policy controller or
published as a private application in Managed Google Play, while ordinary APK sideloading falls under the new
friction. Devices without Google services (AOSP builds) are outside the scheme entirely.

Local schema
                       Target under 30 MB; fonts and the message catalogue ship in the package (no first-run download); images are

                       Every release supports the sync contract and snapshot formats of the two previous releases (N−2); central

6. The version floor

ADR-24 forbids indefinite deferral. The floor is what makes that true without stopping trade.

Aspect                     Design

Definition                 The lowest app version central will accept for sync. Set per release by DevOps; normally N−2.

Grace                      When a release raises the floor, devices below it get a configurable grace period (default 14 days) during which
                           sync continues and the till shows a persistent, non-blocking notice to the supervisor.

After grace                Sync is refused with a clear on-screen reason; selling continues offline (blocking sales would punish the shop
                           for a fleet problem). The device appears in the helpdesk queue automatically. The helpdesk boundary of Q-09 (no
                           connectivity troubleshooting) still applies; the fix is almost always connectivity, and the agent installs as soon as
                           the device reaches central.

Escalation                 A device below the floor for more than the grace period plus 7 days is escalated to the MPCS administrator and
                           the district onboarding team, since a shop that cannot sync for three weeks has a bigger problem than its app
                           version.

What the floor never does It never wipes, locks or disables a till; it never applies to the driver app's proof-of-delivery capture; it never triggers
                                      during the pilot go/no-go window without an explicit decision.
