- **M3-01 and M3-02, the pricing engine on the JVM (27 Sep, branch `feat/m3-01-pricing-engine`).** The shared engine's stubs are replaced by the reference implementation of 23A section 6 (doc 23 section 3.5), pure Kotlin with no clock and no I/O. `Money` gains the fixed rounding (half up to the cent) the engine needs, `Quantity` compares, `Tax.inclusivePart` decomposes a tax-inclusive amount and `Tax.exclusivePart` taxes a trade amount, `Rounding.toRupee` is the receipt's cash adjustment (half up: .49 down, .50 up; the Sprint 0 placeholder did not round .49 down, and `MoneyTest` now says so). `model/` holds the values of 23A section 6 (`RetailLine`, `BatchCandidate`, `Ceiling`, `Policy`, `Rule`, `LineInput`, `LineResult`, `BasketResult`, and `TradeLine`/`TradeQuote` for trade lists); `snapshot/PricingSnapshotIndex` is built once per snapshot version. `PriceResolver.resolveLine` is steps 1 to 4 (list, batch term under AUTO_LOWEST, BARCODE_RESOLVED or PICKER, control term, the lowest with its cap reason); `RuleEvaluator` step 5 (one line rule by PRIORITY_THEN_BEST or BEST_ONLY, doc 23 DR-1; TIME_LIMITED_PRICE, QUANTITY_BREAK, EXPIRY_MARKDOWN on the batch; one BILL_THRESHOLD); `BasketResolver` steps 6 and 7 (re-check against the control term, tax after a pro-rata share of the bill discount, cash rounding). `TradePriceResolver` is what M4 needs (doc 23 section 3.2): the line whose tier is the highest not above the ordered quantity (doc 10 A-03), and the tier check (from 0, rising). `ENGINE_VERSION` is 0.1.0 until the M3-11 freeze tags 1.0.0. Tests: doc 13 scenario 4 reproduced to the cent (931.00, 49.00 off, tax 142.02), one case per step and policy, tiers, rounding .49/.50/.51, and a property test over 10,000 random baskets (no line above a control price or its list price, cap reason consistent, total never above the list, re-resolution identical).
