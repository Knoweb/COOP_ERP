- **K-12 scheduled jobs and K-13 business date (24 Sep, branch `feat/k-12-scheduled-jobs`, on top of K-07).** The job runner of 19A section 12 and the business date of section 13, taken together because the day-close trigger is where they meet, and because M1-09 (the expiry job) and the K-07 gap audit wait on the runner. **Schema** (`kernel/V0051`, lane C): `scheduled_job` (the runtime view of what the code registers; `enabled` is the console's switch and the code never writes it), `job_run` (insert-plus-finish history), `shedlock` (the row lock ShedLock takes per run), `location_business_date` (the day-close state of a location, the template policies). **Runner** (`kernel.api.ScheduledJob` on a bean method: name, cron in the business time zone or continuous with a fixed delay, lockTimeout, maxRuntime, critical; the method takes nothing or a `JobExecution` and returns nothing or the items it processed): `JobRegistry` finds every method as its bean is made and upserts the catalogue at start in every role; `JobScheduler` puts them on the clock in the worker role only; `JobRunner` runs one: disabled skips, a lock held by another instance skips, a run past its maximum is interrupted and marked TIMEOUT with a `JOB_TIMED_OUT` ALERT, a failure is FAILED with a `JOB_FAILED` REVIEW record, a critical job is tried once more at once; the lock (ShedLock's JDBC provider on the application data source, database time) outlives the run. **The system scope, said out loud:** a job has no request behind it and so no scope, yet its ALERT must be an audit record. `coop-erp.system.entity-id` names the entity the platform acts as, the Federation (the local stack sets the dev Federation); in its OWN scope a job records what it must, and with the setting empty it logs instead. `SystemScope.federationView()` is the read-only view a fleet-wide job (gap check, cut-off) reads with. The kernel's own jobs moved onto the runner: `partition-maintenance` (daily), `idempotency-expiry` (hourly, as 19A lists it; was daily), `gap-check` (nightly, critical, now recording `NUMBERING_GAP` per gap), `day-close-cutoff`. The outbox relay keeps its own loop: it is K-05's, runs every second, and a lock row per poll is the wrong price; its owner decides. **Business date** (K-13): `LocationBusinessDates` replaces the 17A calendar stub behind `BusinessDate`: a location with no row trades on the calendar date; `DayClose.close` advances it to the day after the one closed, or to today when the location was shut longer, once per calendar day at most, with `DAY_CLOSED` and `location.day_closed.v1`. `DayCloseTrigger` consumes `till_session.closed.v1` and closes the day when the payload says `openSessionsRemaining` is zero (the contract with M6, 26A SessionHook: the kernel reads no module table, so M6 says it in the event); the cut-off job closes every location still behind at 02:30. **Not here:** the manual run from the fleet console (`sys.job.run`, MFA: K-02/K-03), the per-location cut-off (a configuration item, K-11), the till clock offset (`ClockOffset`, with K-08 heartbeats). **Proof** (`JobRunnerPostgresIntegrationTest`, `DayClosePostgresIntegrationTest`): the code registers its jobs and leaves the switch alone; a run is recorded with what it processed; two instances make one run per schedule and the next schedule runs again; a run past its maximum is interrupted, TIMEOUT, alerted under the system entity; a failure is FAILED and a critical job retried once, with the REVIEW records; a location trades on the calendar date until its first close; a close advances once per day and tells everybody; a location shut for a week reopens on today; the cut-off closes every location behind; the trigger is bound to the session event.
