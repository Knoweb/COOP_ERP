## What was open

Seven points the reviews of K-06, K-09, K-10 and K-13 (#97, #125, #127, #132, #137, #139) recorded as deviations or left for the architect:

1. `kernel.notification_pending` held the recipient of a QUEUED notification (a phone number) and its placeholders in clear for up to 24 hours. Doc 19 §7: "phone numbers are hashed in the log". The placeholders of M1's direct send are a temporary password.
2. An attachment settles only after its pre-signed PUT expires (fifteen minutes) and on the verifier's next run (every five), so up to twenty minutes after the upload; a write-off approval that needs the photograph (doc 32 §4) waits that long.
3. Nothing deletes the object of an upload that failed verification, for a document's attachment or a module's object, nor a module's settled object once nothing refers to it.
4. `NfcConverter` applies to every String attribute, where 19A §6 names a marker type `I18nText`.
5. Whether the kernel carries an e-mail adapter against the compose Mailpit now, or every provider adapter waits for M9 (19A §10).
6. The business date of a document with no location, issued by someone acting entity-wide: 19A §13 says "never from the wall clock", but an entity has no day close.
7. The A4/PDF renderer (Thymeleaf, headless Chromium, bundled Noto fonts) was moved out of K-06 to M4 on 21 September, on the reasoning "nothing prints before M4".

## Decisions and the change, as applied

1. **Sealed, not in clear.** What a retry needs is sealed with AES-256-GCM in the application under `coop-erp.notification.pending-key` (base64 of 32 bytes; no default outside development, like the idempotency secret), a fresh nonce per row and the notification id as associated data; the row stores the ciphertext and a key id. `pending-key-previous` opens rows of the old key for the 24 hours a row can live. Kernel V0063 drops the clear columns `recipient` and `arguments` and adds `sealed` and `key_id`. Rejected: pgcrypto (the key would travel in SQL text, where the statement log and `pg_stat_statements` keep it); re-resolving the audience at retry (does not cover a direct send, and needs the event from the archive); a shorter retention (still clear text, and the 24-hour retry window is doc 19's). **Doc 19 §7, Privacy, add:** "What a retry needs (the recipient and the placeholders) is kept until the notification is settled, at most 24 hours, encrypted under a key held outside the database, and cleared when settled." **19A §10, add** the same sentence under "Retry and suppression".
2. **The rule stays; both halves are configuration.** A row settles only after its upload URL has expired (the bytes cannot change after the hash is checked). The URL's validity is the register's `attachment.upload_url_minutes` (FEDERATION, 1 to 60, default 15; the read URL keeps `coop-erp.object-store.presign-minutes`), and the verifier's schedule is `coop-erp.attachment.verify-cron` (every five minutes by default). Lowering both brings the wait to a few minutes without new code. Rejected for now: an "upload complete" call with a server-side copy to an immutable key (the copy is plain S3 and would allow settling at once, but it changes the key a document's attachment is read from and belongs with the one screen that needs it); object lock (provider-specific). Carried as a follow-up for M5's write-off approval (`docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md` 6.14). **19A §9, Presign:** "(15 minutes)" reads "(the register's `attachment.upload_url_minutes`, 15 by default)"; **Verification:** "every 5 minutes" reads "every 5 minutes by default (`coop-erp.attachment.verify-cron`), for PENDING rows whose upload URL has expired".
3. **A FAILED upload's file is deleted after a retention; the kernel never deletes a verified one.** A new K-12 job, `attachment-cleanup` (nightly, `coop-erp.attachment.cleanup-cron`, 03:40 by default), deletes the object of a FAILED `document_attachment` or `object_upload` row settled more than `attachment.failed_retention_days` ago (FEDERATION, default 30), at most `attachment.cleanup.batch_size` of each kind per run (500), and marks the row `object_deleted_at`, audited `ATTACHMENT_OBJECT_DELETED` or `OBJECT_DELETED`. Kernel V0063 lets that be the one change a settled row admits, once, on a FAILED row only; `document_attachment` also gains `settled_at`, written by the verifier. A settled module object nothing refers to any more is the module's to report (the kernel cannot know a module's references): a follow-up (`docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md` 6.15), with the PENDING ledger rows a module never verifies. **19A §9, add** "Clean-up" as above; **§12**, the phase-1 job seed gains `attachment-cleanup (nightly)`; **doc 18 part C**, `document_attachment` gains `settled_at` and `object_deleted_at`.
4. **NFC on every String stays.** A marker a module forgets on one column is the column that ends up with two spellings of one name; NFC on a code, an id or base64 changes nothing. No `I18nText` type is added. **19A §6, Normalisation:** "NfcConverter as a JPA AttributeConverter applied to every String attribute (`@Converter(autoApply = true)`); the till applies the same rule in Kotlin."
5. **No provider adapter in the kernel.** 19A §10 names `NotificationChannel` "M9's adapter interface" and doc 19 §7 gives the providers to M9; a kernel adapter, even a development-only one, would be a second home for provider code and a coupling the interface exists to prevent. The tests use their own channels; until M9 there is no rule, and M1's temporary password is returned in the command's result. M9's first ticket builds the EMAIL adapter against the compose Mailpit. No document changes.
6. **An entity has no business date of its own.** Nothing closes an entity's day, so a document with no location, issued entity-wide, takes the calendar date in the business time zone, as `IssuanceProtocol` already does; a document that concerns one location (a GRN, a count, a write-off, a discrepancy raised on a GRN) names that location and takes that location's date. Rejected: an entity business date (a second day-close with no event to close it); requiring ORD or DISC to name a location (24A's `doc_order` has no location, and an order is the buying entity's). **19A §13, Business date, add:** "A document with no location (an entity-level order, invoice, credit note, payment receipt) takes the calendar date in the business time zone at issuance; an entity has no day close. A document that concerns one location names it." **Doc 18 part C, `business_date`:** "from the location's day-close; for a document with no location, the calendar date in the business time zone." **24A:** a DISC raised on a GRN carries the GRN's location.
7. **The renderer comes before M3-10, not M4.** 23A names "A4Renderer for labels" in its kernel dependencies and M3-10's label job prints shelf labels through it (`LabelRenderer uses A4Renderer`), and M3 is built before M4, so "nothing prints before M4" was wrong. The renderer becomes kernel ticket K-06b, due before M3-10 and owned by the platform pair: Thymeleaf to PDF through Playwright's headless Chromium in the worker role only, the three Noto families the web client already uses (Noto Sans, Noto Sans Sinhala, Noto Sans Tamil, SIL OFL) bundled with the backend, output to object storage, `report.rendered.v1`, and the golden trilingual PDF test of 19A §6. No module before M3 prints (M1 and M2 print nothing; weigh labels and batch stickers are M6's peripherals). About two days; **`docs/PLAN_TO_M2.md`, the 21 September decision** reads as above.
